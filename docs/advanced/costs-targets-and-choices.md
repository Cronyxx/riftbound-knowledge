# Costs, targets, and choices

::: warning Draft — awaiting review
For two-player Duel. Sources checked November 14, 2026.
:::

## Which decisions happen before a response?

During finalization, select modes, targets, relevant destinations, and optional additional costs; determine the total cost; pay it; then check legality. These decisions happen before the ordinary opportunity to react to the finalized spell or ability. ([Core Rules][core], 353–359.)

## Pay costs in the right order

Energy and Power are paid before non-standard costs such as killing or discarding something. Reaction abilities that Add resources can be used at the appropriate cost-payment step. A cost paid to play a spell is not refunded merely because the spell is later countered. ([Core Rules][core], 357.1–357.2; 426.1.c; 430.3.)

**Official case:** You play Atakhan and elect to kill a non-token unit as its additional cost. You cannot banish that same unit with Mordekaiser, Iron Revenant to fund Atakhan's Power cost: it has not reached the trash when you pay Power. ([Radiance FAQ][faq], Atakhan/Mordekaiser question; rules 357.1–357.2.)

## A choice is not always a target

Choosing a specific object is generally targeting, including instructions that omit the literal word “choose.” Picking is not targeting. Exceptions include instructions affecting an entire group and certain choices made by another player. Read the targeting rules before assuming that every decision is locked in at finalization. ([Core Rules][core], 355.5–355.10.)

For example, an instruction requiring each player to kill one of their own units makes each player pick during resolution. An instruction targeting one particular unit requires its target earlier. ([Core Rules][core], 355.10.e.)

## What if the target changes?

Recheck legality as the effect resolves. An illegal target is unaffected; the spell still resolves and follows its remaining applicable instructions. Do not replace an invalid target with a new one without explicit permission. ([Core Rules][core], 355.15; 359.3.e.1–359.3.e.8.)

**Worked case:** Void Seeker targets a unit at a battlefield. A legal Reaction moves that unit to base before Void Seeker resolves. Its battlefield target is now illegal, so it deals no damage to that unit; the independent draw instruction still happens. This example is included in rule 359.3.e.5. For linked instructions, see [Effects, exceptions, and cleanup](/advanced/effects-and-cleanup).
