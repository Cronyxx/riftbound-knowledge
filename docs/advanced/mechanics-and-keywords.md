# Mechanics and keywords

::: warning Draft — awaiting review
For two-player Duel. Sources checked November 14, 2026. This first mechanics batch covers Action, Reaction, Hidden, and buffs.

## What does a keyword let you do?

Use the chain, timing, choice, and cleanup procedures from the preceding articles to read these mechanics. Start with the Action and Reaction permissions you have already used, then apply them to Hidden and buff counters.

Keywords can grant permission, change a procedure, or refer to a game object. Read the keyword together with the rest of the card: timing permission does not waive costs or targeting requirements.

| Term | How to use it | Reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action | Play the card or activate that ability during an open showdown on either player's turn, as well as at its ordinary timing. It does not permit a response to an existing chain. | [Core Rules][core], 806; 308–310 |
| Reaction | Includes Action's permissions and allows play during a closed state, when a chain exists. You still need an opportunity to act. | [Core Rules][core], 813; 312 |
| Hidden | Permits hiding the card now and playing it from facedown starting next turn. Hiding and playing are separate actions. | [Core Rules][core], 811 |
| Buff | A counter that normally adds 1 Might. A unit normally has at most one buff; another instruction to buff it does not add a second. | [Core Rules][core], 701–705 |

## Hiding a card, then playing it

On your turn in a Neutral Open state, pay one Power of any domain to hide a card with Hidden from your hand or Champion Zone at a battlefield you control with an available facedown space. Hiding does not start a chain. Beginning next turn, the hidden card gains Reaction and can be played ignoring its base cost. Additional costs still matter. ([Core Rules][core], 422; 811.1; 356–357.)

A hidden permanent normally enters at that battlefield. Targets of a spell played from facedown, and of a hidden permanent's play effect, normally come from that battlefield. Explicit targeting restrictions can create exceptions; evaluate each target separately. Losing control removes your facedown cards there during cleanup. ([Core Rules][core], 811.1.d–811.2; 323.6.a.)

## Practice the distinction

You hide a card on your turn. You cannot immediately play it using Hidden's next-turn permission. On the opponent's turn, check the battlefield, legal targets, additional costs, and your reaction opportunity before revealing and playing it.

A temporary Might increase is not automatically a buff counter. Track the counter separately from effects that last this turn. ([Core Rules][core], 701–705; 479.4.)
