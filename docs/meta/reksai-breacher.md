## Game plan: make the next unit matter now

Rek’Sai combines Fury’s attacking pressure with Order’s extra bodies and resource tools. The list studied here uses **Rek’Sai, Breacher** as its Chosen Champion. Breacher gives friendly units played from outside a player’s hand access to **Accelerate**, an optional additional payment that lets a unit enter ready. Tokens and plays enabled by the Legend can therefore become immediate battlefield threats when you can afford the extra costs.

Your goal is to turn an early board into a Conquer, then use the Legend’s access to more cards to maintain pressure. **Conquer** means taking control of a battlefield; [Scoring and winning](/basics/scoring) explains the scoring limits. Cheap units let you threaten multiple locations, while **Undertitan** can turn several surviving bodies into a much stronger attacking turn.

The deck rewards resource planning. A new body is not automatically a ready attacker, an attacking bonus does not protect a defender, and a good reveal is only useful if you can pay for the play it offers.

## Key cards and packages

### Breacher and the Legend

[Breacher, SFD-029](https://riftbound.org/cards/sfd-spiritforged/sfd-029-221/reksai-breacher), has three printed Might, Accelerate, and Assault. **Assault** increases Might while attacking; Breacher’s unnumbered Assault grants one. Its other ability grants Accelerate to friendly units played from anywhere other than a player’s hand. This lets you choose between conserving resources and paying to make an eligible unit useful immediately. See [Core Rules 807, Assault](/reference/core-rules#rule-807), and [805, Accelerate](/reference/core-rules#rule-805).

**Void Burrower** triggers when you conquer: you may exhaust the Legend to reveal two cards, optionally banish one and play it, then recycle the rest. Follow the updated wording in Riot’s [Spiritforged Errata, “Void Burrower”](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/rules-and-releases/riftbound-spiritforged-errata/), January 14, 2026. It grants access to a play; it does not waive that card’s costs or grant a special battlefield destination. Budget for the revealed card and any Accelerate payment before committing your resources elsewhere.

### Bodies that support another play

[Faithful Manufactor](https://riftbound.org/cards/ogn-origins/ogn-211-298/faithful-manufactor) costs three Energy and brings a one-Might Recruit to its location when played. [Carrion Dredger](https://riftbound.org/cards/unl-unleashed/unl-153-219/carrion-dredger) leaves a one-Might Bird with Deflect in base through **Deathknell**, a trigger when it dies. These extra bodies help you maintain a board and provide expendable pieces for later effects.

[Concert Poster](https://riftbound.org/cards/rad-radiance/rad-116-167/concert-poster) has **Deploy**, so it is played to a battlefield and is killed when an opponent holds there. On play or when you conquer at its location, it offers a one-Energy payment for a Recruit in base. Poster therefore asks two questions: can you use the body, and can you defend or repeatedly contest its location? See [Core Rules 830, Deploy](/reference/core-rules#rule-830).

[Shimmering Swimmer](https://riftbound.org/cards/rad-radiance/rad-014-167/shimmering-swimmer) can kill a friendly unit or gear as an additional cost; paying it enables a play effect dealing two damage to a unit. Trading an expendable body for useful damage can clear a scoring route. Avoid sacrificing the unit you still need to defend a point merely because the extra effect is available.

[Inferna](https://riftbound.org/cards/unl-unleashed/unl-002-219/inferna) supplies a different timing option: **Ambush** lets it be played as a Reaction to a battlefield where you have units. Its Assault 2 adds attacking Might, but it still enters exhausted unless an applicable effect changes that. Reserve it for an opportunity where its presence changes the exchange; do not count its attacking bonus while defending.

### Selection, removal, and a closing turn

[Trifarian Captain](https://riftbound.org/cards/rad-radiance/rad-129-167/trifarian-captain) looks at four cards on play and can draw a revealed unit or gear costing at most three Energy and one Power. This is selection into hand, rather than a free deployment. [Noxus Hopeful](https://riftbound.org/cards/ogn-origins/ogn-012-298/noxus-hopeful) becomes two Energy cheaper through **Legion**, which checks whether you have played another card that turn. Plan the first card before deciding how much a follow-up costs.

[Bilgewater Tracker](https://riftbound.org/cards/rad-radiance/rad-003-167/bilgewater-tracker) deals one damage to each of up to two units on play. Falling Star supplies two three-damage instructions; their targets are committed before responses, under Riot’s [Spiritforged FAQ, “Falling Star, Icathian Rain, and Deflect”](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/rules-and-releases/riftbound-spiritforged-faq/), January 14, 2026. [Cull the Weak](https://riftbound.org/cards/ogn-origins/ogn-209-298/cull-the-weak) makes each player kill one of their own units: the opponent can give up a spare body instead of their best threat.

[Undertitan](https://riftbound.org/cards/sfd-spiritforged/sfd-175-221/undertitan) adds two Energy as it is revealed from your deck, and its play effect gives your other units two extra Might for the turn. Merely looking at it does not produce that Energy; Captain only reveals the card you select. The reveal effect is a **replacement effect**, modifying an event as it happens, rather than a later trigger you must wait to resolve. See the Undertitan example in [Core Rules 370.1.b](/reference/core-rules#rule-370-1-b). A wide board makes its play effect more valuable, but that board still needs a viable scoring route.

## Opening decisions

**Strategic judgment:** look for early bodies, a useful follow-up, and enough flexibility to avoid investing everything in one attacker. Breacher is already accessible as the Chosen Champion, so an opening hand should support its plan rather than consist entirely of expensive payoffs.

| Example hand | Decision to consider |
| --- | --- |
| Carrion Dredger, Trifarian Captain, Noxus Hopeful, Falling Star | A candidate keep: early development, selection, and interaction. Choose a sequence that enables Hopeful without losing the needed answer. |
| Undertitan, Undertitan, Ferrous Forerunner, Imperial Decree | Look to replace expensive or situational cards for early development. A powerful late turn does not help if the opponent controls the scoring pace first. |
| Concert Poster, Faithful Manufactor, Shimmering Swimmer, Cull the Weak | A coherent body-and-sacrifice plan, but check whether spending on setup leaves you able to contest. Do not sacrifice your only effective attacker. |

Treat these as exercises, not automatic keeps. Opposing removal, battlefield choices, and available domain payments can change the answer. Start with the source’s 7/5 Rune split and record failed payments before altering it.

## Playing the deck

**Early:** establish a body and plan Breacher’s timing. **Middle:** use ready units and efficient trades to earn a Conquer without exhausting every resource before the Legend’s opportunity. **Late:** identify the point that wins or prevents a loss; use Undertitan’s temporary boost when the extra Might changes that exchange.

### Worked sequence: Manufactor into a ready Recruit

Assume Breacher is already on your board, Manufactor is in hand, you have four Energy and one Power available, and no cost modifiers or opposing responses apply. You are at a legal opportunity to play a unit to base.

1. Pay three Energy to play Manufactor to base. It came from hand and does not gain Accelerate from Breacher.
2. Its play trigger instructs you to play a Recruit there. The token is played outside the hand, so Breacher grants it Accelerate.
3. Choose to pay one Energy and one Power of any domain for that domainless Recruit’s Accelerate. It enters ready. Without that optional payment, it enters exhausted.
4. When the chain clears and you have a legal Standard Move opportunity, consider moving the ready Recruit to a battlefield. Entering ready does not itself move it or guarantee a point.

This costs **four Energy and one Power**, with Manufactor and the Recruit ending in base before movement. Token play follows [Core Rules 185.2.a](/reference/core-rules#rule-185-2-a); domainless Recruit traits and Accelerate payment are covered by [187.1](/reference/core-rules#rule-187-1) and [805.1.b.2](/reference/core-rules#rule-805-1-b-2). Review [Movement](/basics/movement) and [Chains and resolution](/advanced/chains-and-resolution) for the subsequent opportunities.

Opposing Reactions can change the setup. Removing Breacher before the token’s Accelerate choice can remove that option. Once Accelerate has been paid during finalization, losing the keyword does not undo that payment’s ready-entry effect: [Core Rules 805.2.b](/reference/core-rules#rule-805-2-b). Count the resources and response windows rather than treating the whole sequence as one uninterruptible action.

## Weaknesses and counters

**Strategic analysis, checked November 25, 2026.** The counter tools below are legal under the checked ban list, but must fit the opponent’s domains and scoring plan.

| Weakness | Opponent’s counterplay | Rek’Sai pilot’s mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breacher enables immediate pressure from later plays. | Remove it before the next token or non-hand unit is prepared, forcing slower development. | Maintain an ordinary development line and avoid a turn that only works with Breacher surviving. |
| Many useful bodies have low printed Might. | Use efficient damage to remove multiple pieces before a closing turn. | Keep a follow-up and weigh spreading threats against exposing everything to one answer. |
| Accelerate consumes resources beyond the normal play. | Force payment on one location, then contest another while resources are low. | Pay only when being ready changes a score, a defense, or a valuable attack. |
| Some resources depend on conquering. | Deny the first successful contest instead of letting every small attacker trade for a point and a Legend opportunity. | Preserve an alternative battlefield and avoid spending all resources before a reveal. |
| Poster must survive at a battlefield to keep contributing. | Remove the gear or establish a hold at its location. | Treat it as one source of bodies rather than the whole game plan. |

### Counter tools and their limits

**Falling Star in Fury:** its two damage instructions can punish a board containing Breacher and a small support unit at a legal spell-play opportunity. It can also focus on one larger unit. The opponent can react after targets are committed; do not assume six damage guarantees two kills. Killing Dredger may still leave its Bird, so select targets for the scoring exchange rather than the largest apparent card trade. See Riot’s [Falling Star FAQ section](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/rules-and-releases/riftbound-spiritforged-faq/).

**Salvage in Order:** this optionally kills a gear and draws a card, making Concert Poster a possible target. It has Action timing, so it cannot be used as a Reaction to interrupt a trigger already on the chain. Removing Poster limits future opportunities; it does not remove its existing Recruits. Current wording: Riot’s [Origins Errata, “Salvage”](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/news/rules-and-releases/riftbound-origins-card-errata/), October 28, 2025. See [Timing and reaction opportunities](/advanced/timing-and-reactions).

### Turn disruption into a point

Suppose an opposing Fury deck has an opportunity to remove Breacher before your next development turn, and already has a unit ready to contest. After doing so, it can pressure a location while your next token enters exhausted unless another effect supplies readiness. Its advantage comes from the changed scoring window. If it spends the whole turn removing support and never contests, your normal unit plays may rebuild the board.

Neither named spell is a demonstrated hard-counter matchup. Test whether it changes the next point, how often the required timing occurs, and what including it costs your own deck.

## Practice and common mistakes

1. **Token budget:** with Breacher and Poster available, calculate the payment for the Recruit offer separately from Accelerate. Decide whether a ready Recruit earns anything this turn.
2. **Reveal versus look:** compare revealing Undertitan through the Legend with merely seeing it in Captain’s four cards. Identify exactly when it is revealed and whether you can use the added Energy.
3. **Sacrifice choice:** with Dredger, a Recruit, and Breacher on board, explain which unit you would give up to Cull or Swimmer, including the battlefield you still need to defend.
4. **Reserve a follow-up:** plan an attack that wins a point while keeping a useful play if the first attacker dies.

Common mistakes are paying Accelerate without a purpose, assuming the Legend plays cards for free, treating Assault as defensive Might, sacrificing the last relevant defender, and committing every body before a possible clear. Record the decisive scoring turn alongside the cards drawn.
