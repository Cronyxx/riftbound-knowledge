## Game plan: turn Equipment into a board

**Emperor of the Sands**, the Calm/Order Legend, gives your Sand Soldiers **Weaponmaster**: a triggered ability that can attach Equipment when a unit is played. After you play an Equipment, the Legend can spend one Energy and exhaust to play a 2 Might Sand Soldier to your base. Equipment therefore develops your future attackers while also enabling bodies now.

The payoff is **Arise!**, which plays a Sand Soldier for each Equipment you control and then creates a separate effect to ready up to two of them. Build enough gear to make that investment worthwhile, then threaten a battlefield with ready Soldiers or attack with **Azir, Sovereign**, whose attack ability can bring your token units to his battlefield. Do not commit every body to a fight that leaves the opponent an uncontested scoring location. See [scoring and winning](/basics/scoring) for the distinction between taking control and earning the final point.

This exact list has **16 Equipment, three printed units, and 21 spells**, counting the Chosen Champion within the 40-card Main Deck. Most of its army must be generated. Drawing protection without a body, or bodies without useful gear, is the central consistency risk.

## The important interactions

### Equipment and Sand Soldiers

A Sand Soldier is a domainless 2 Might unit token ([Core Rules 187.3](/reference/core-rules#rule-187-3)). Playing a token triggers effects that look for a unit being played, but a token is not a card. The Legend’s condition specifically asks whether you played an **Equipment**, so moving an existing Equipment between units does not satisfy it.

The low-cost gear—**Doran’s Shield, Eye of the Herald, and Soul Sword**—helps you establish the engine without spending an entire turn on one expensive card. **Brutalizer, Hand Hammer, and B.F. Sword** fill out the equipment density. Judge a gear play by what it enables this turn and what it contributes to a later Arise!, rather than treating a high Equipment count as a score by itself.

Weaponmaster offers an attachment at the Equip cost reduced by **one Power of any domain**. This discount can reduce a domain-specific Power cost: **Doran’s Shield’s one-Calm-Power Equip cost becomes zero**, so the Soldier can attach it without spending Power. The Any Power symbol does not restrict the discount to costs printed with that same symbol. The official domain-specific discount example in [356.4.f.1](/reference/core-rules#rule-356-4-f-1) demonstrates this. Weaponmaster does not erase Energy or non-resource costs; a cost with no Power receives no Power discount ([821.1.c](/reference/core-rules#rule-821-1-c)). You may select already attached Equipment, subject to legal attachment and payment; moving it strengthens one Soldier by taking it away from another. See [821.1.b–c](/reference/core-rules#rule-821-1-b) for the full procedure.

### Arise!, Guards!, and current wording

The January 14 **Spiritforged Errata** changes Arise! to play a Soldier for each Equipment you control, followed by **“Then do this: Ready up to two of them.”** That second instruction is a **reflexive trigger**: a separate triggered ability placed on the chain, the area where pending spells and abilities wait to resolve. Weaponmaster triggers and this readying effect must be handled as separate items, with the normal response opportunities. Do not resolve the whole card as one uninterrupted attachment-and-attack instruction ([Core Rules 388–389](/reference/core-rules#rule-388)).

**Guards!** supplies a smaller body-producing play and has Hidden, which supports preparing a card face down for later use; see the [Hidden study](/mechanics/hidden). April 3 **Unleashed Errata** changes its follow-up to **“Then do this: You may pay [Order Power] to ready it.”** Budget for that payment rather than assuming the Soldier enters ready.

### Combat support and interaction

**Discipline** adds 2 Might for the turn and draws a card. **Battle Preparations** also adds 2 Might; showing off gear can ready up to two runes. Plan which payment those runes will enable before spending the spell. **Deathgrip** trades a friendly unit for a temporary Might increase on another friendly unit and a card. Its updated text kills the first unit during resolution; the kill is not an additional cost paid when announcing the spell.

**Defy** counters a spell only within its printed Energy and Power limits. **Hidden Blade** kills a unit at a battlefield but lets that unit’s controller draw two cards: use it when the position or point matters more than the cards you give back. **Back Off**, **Kennen, Keeper of Balance**, and **Vi, Peacekeeper** provide ways to stun units, preventing their combat damage that turn. Stun alone does not remove a blocker or guarantee control of the battlefield.

## Opening decisions

These are testing recommendations, not the pilot’s published mulligan guide. Prefer an opening with cheap Equipment and a realistic first Soldier over an expensive payoff with no setup. Keep one useful interaction when the opponent can pressure early; avoid retaining several support spells that need a friendly unit you cannot produce.

| Example four-card hand | Suggested approach |
| --- | --- |
| Doran’s Shield, Soul Sword, Discipline, Arise! | Keep the cheap setup. Consider replacing Arise! when immediate pressure is likely; it is a later payoff, not your first body. |
| B.F. Sword, Arise!, Hidden Blade, Deathgrip | Replace the expensive engine pieces first. This hand has ways to spend resources but no cheap Equipment to enable the Legend early. |
| Eye of the Herald, Guards!, Defy, Battle Preparations | A functional setup-and-response hand. Reserve resources for a body before assuming both support spells will be available. |

Early, establish gear while keeping enough Energy for the Legend. In the middle turns, choose between widening the board and committing Azir to a scoring fight. Late, ask whether another Equipment meaningfully improves Arise! or whether holding interaction better protects a point.

### Worked sequence: one Equipment, one Soldier

Start in an open state during your turn with a ready Emperor of the Sands, Doran’s Shield in hand, at least two Energy available, and no effects increasing Equip costs. No Power is needed for this sequence. Assume no opposing response changes the board.

1. Play Doran’s Shield for one Energy and let it resolve. You have now played an Equipment this turn.
2. Spend one Energy and exhaust the Legend to activate its token ability. The opponent can respond through the normal chain procedure; once it resolves, play the Soldier to your base.
3. The Soldier’s Weaponmaster ability triggers. On resolution, you may choose the Shield and attach it: its one-Calm-Power Equip cost is reduced to zero by Weaponmaster. No Calm Power is spent.
4. The complete sequence costs two Energy and exhausts the Legend, with no Power payment. The Soldier has not been readied by these effects. Preserve it for later development rather than treating it as an immediate standard-move attacker.

Azir’s **Accelerate** is a different route to immediate action: an additional one Energy and one Order Power lets him enter ready. Budget that on top of his four-Energy play cost ([Core Rules 805](/reference/core-rules#rule-805)).

## Battlefields and sideboarding

**Forge of the Fluft** gives your Legend an exhaust ability to attach Equipment. That competes with exhausting the same Legend to make a Soldier. **Hall of Legends** can ready your Legend after conquering there if you pay one Energy; another activation still needs its normal costs and conditions. **Trifarian War Camp** gives units there +1 Might, including opposing units. Recalculate both sides before relying on it.

The source supplies sideboard contents, not a sideboarding guide. Treat these roles as hypotheses: **Disarming Rake** and extra **Salvage** address gear; **Charm** changes positioning; extra **Vi** offers another stun threat. **Decree of Focus** is conditional protection against Fury interaction, while **Decree of Unity** removes an enemy Chaos unit or gear. **Hush** counters spells costing at least two Power; it cannot replace Defy against every cheap spell.

For practice, replace cards whose targets are absent before cutting cheap Equipment. Reducing the gear count weakens both the Legend’s activation condition and Arise!’s output. Record each swap rather than presenting an invented fixed in/out map as the original pilot’s plan.

## Weaknesses and counters

The following are tactical plans to test, not statistically demonstrated counter matchups.

| Weakness | Opponent’s counterplay | Azir pilot’s mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gear setup costs time | Pressure an exposed battlefield while Azir invests in Equipment. Make a support spell defend a point before the large token turn. | Develop the cheapest functional engine; hold off on expensive gear when a Soldier or interaction prevents a score. |
| Arise! depends on Equipment in play | Remove gear before the payoff resolves. Order’s **Salvage** is an Action that can kill a gear and draw a card; Calm’s **Disarming Rake** can kill gear with its play effect. | Keep enough body production outside Arise!, and avoid assuming the current Equipment count is guaranteed. |
| Several buffs require a friendly unit | Disrupt the intended attacker rather than fighting the buffed army on its best terms. Force protection on one battlefield, then contest another. | Spread threats when scoring permits; do not spend all protection on a unit that cannot earn or deny a point. |
| A large spell concentrates investment | Calm’s **Wind Wall** can counter Arise! as a spell. Countering the spell prevents that token wave, but does not counter the Legend’s separate activated ability. | Seek a smaller scoring line first when the opponent leaves counterspell resources available. Preserve another way to make bodies. |
| Concentrating tokens opens other locations | Present threats across battlefields instead of feeding every unit into Azir’s gathered force. | Use Azir’s attack movement selectively. Bringing every token is optional. |

Timing matters: Salvage is not a Reaction, so it cannot simply be inserted into a response window to Arise!. Use its legal Action opportunity beforehand. Removing one Equipment reduces the next Arise! by one Soldier; it does not switch off every other token source. Conversely, an opposing **Defy** cannot counter Arise!: Arise!’s six-Energy cost exceeds Defy’s limit.

For a practical counterplay line, remove an Equipment during a legal Action opportunity, then pressure the battlefield Azir would otherwise defend with the next token wave. The removal matters because it helps earn or deny a point. Spending a turn attacking irrelevant gear while Azir scores elsewhere is not effective counterplay.

Against fast pressure, test whether your setup can defend before the first substantial token turn. Against spell control, test smaller deployments that make the opponent commit interaction before Arise!. Against another Equipment deck, compare the point gained by removal with the tempo lost playing it. No favorable or unfavorable matchup percentage is established by the available event record.

## Practice and review

- With two cheap Equipment in hand, identify whether playing both leaves Energy for the Legend and Power for attachment.
- Before Arise!, count Equipment still in play, then name the distinct token-play, Weaponmaster, and readying effects you will resolve.
- With Forge of the Fluft available, explain why attaching gear is worth giving up the Legend’s token activation this turn.
- After each loss, record whether the bottleneck was Equipment, token access, colored Power, or a scoring decision. Track sideboard swaps separately from game results.
