## Proposed deck-study format

1. **Game plan at a glance:** what the deck tries to do and its intended route to victory.
2. **Context and list:** format, region, event or source, result, date, legend, chosen champion, domains, and full list.
3. **Key cards and packages:** connect the cards to that plan and explain which substitutions change it.
4. **Opening decisions:** mulligan priorities and example hands, explained as strategic judgments.
5. **Playing the deck:** early-, middle-, and late-game priorities, sequencing, and important mechanics, with sourced rules explanations.
6. **Battlefields and sideboarding:** choices where applicable to the selected format and list.
7. **Matchups:** strengths, weaknesses, and adjustments, distinguishing results from hypotheses.
8. **Practice:** common mistakes and situations to work through.
9. **Sources and review date:** list provenance, competitive evidence, rules references, and legality checks.

## Evidence before labels

A deck will only be described as part of the meta when dated competitive evidence supports that description. A single list can be studied without claiming it is a leading deck. Win rates and matchup claims need a named dataset, period, and sample size; otherwise they remain clearly labeled analysis or open questions.

Rules and legality come from [Riot’s Rules Hub](https://playriftbound.com/en-us/rules-hub/). Tournament results and decklists support competitive observations; the rulebook does not establish which decks are strongest.
